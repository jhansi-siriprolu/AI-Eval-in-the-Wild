Perplexity, hands-on

Perplexity measures how "surprised" a model is by real text. Move the sliders below — each one is the model's predicted probability for the token that actually came next — and watch the score respond live.

Sentence: "The capital of France is ___." — five candidate next-token probabilities a model might assign:

—perplexity over these 5 tokens

The formula

PPL = exp( -(1/N) × Σ log P(token_i) )

In words: take the model's predicted probability for each actual next token, average their logs, negate, and exponentiate. A model that's certain and correct about every token scores close to 1. A model guessing uniformly among V possible tokens scores close to V.

PPL ≈ 1–5

Model is confidently, correctly predicting the text — typical for a well-trained model on text similar to its training data.

PPL ≈ 20–50

Model is meaningfully uncertain — common on out-of-domain or unusual text (e.g. code for a model trained mostly on prose).

PPL > 100

Model is close to guessing randomly for this text — a red flag for a domain mismatch or broken tokenization.

Try the extremes yourself

Drag every slider to 100% — perplexity should drop to almost exactly 1.0 (a perfectly confident, correct model). Then drag every slider down to 20% (uniform guessing among 5 options) — perplexity should land near 5.0, exactly the number of options. This is the calculation lm-evaluation-harness runs internally, just applied to one word choice instead of a whole benchmark.
